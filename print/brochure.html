An open-source graphical framework for the design, simulation and evaluation of Optical Quantum Data Centers — circuit, network, compilation, scheduling and execution in one environment.

What a run tells you

Five numbers that change when the network changes.

Entanglement fidelity
Quality of the pairs actually delivered to remote gates.
Job execution time (JET)
Wall-clock length of the distributed job, network included.
Communication overhead
Time and resources spent moving information, not computing.
Entanglement-resource utilization
How hard generation and memory resources are worked.
Penalty delay
Waiting caused by a remote gate outrunning its entangled pair.

The gap

The tools for quantum data center research don't talk to each other.

Circuit frameworks, distributed compilers and network execution environments each solve one layer well. Nothing connects them, so the question that matters most — how the optical network changes what the application actually achieves — falls between them.

Circuit frameworks

Qiskit · PyTket · QASM

Strong circuit design and compilation, but a single-device view. The interconnect isn't part of the model.

Distributed compilers

pytket-dqc

Partition circuits across modules and allocate resources, but the communication they assume stays abstract — nothing ties the partition back to real fiber.

Network execution

Qoala · NetSquid

Rigorous node scheduling and protocol-level modelling of quantum communication, with no notion of the circuit that produced the traffic.

QNEST

The connective tissue

Rather than reimplementing any layer, QNEST wires these tools into one workflow and adds what was missing: optical network design, switching protocols, and a graphical environment over the whole run.

The workflow

Five stages, in order, end to end.

Each stage hands its output to the next one.

  1. 01 Circuit · MQT Bench

    Choose the benchmark circuit and the number of qubits for the run, and set the duration of every gate and process.

  2. 02 Network

    Define the topology and the connectivity between QPUs: beam splitters, switches, quantum memories and optical links, with qubit counts, T1 and T2, gate and readout errors.

  3. 03 Distribute · pytket-dqc

    pytket-dqc compiles the circuit against that network and distributes it over the QPUs, exposing remote-gate dependencies and entanglement demand.

  4. 04 Schedule · Qoala + NetSquid

    The distributed circuit is passed to the QPUs and scheduled by strategies defined in Qoala and NetSquid, then drawn as an interactive Gantt chart exported as standalone HTML.

  5. 05 Run · switching protocols

    Our switching protocols are applied for either an all-photonic or a memory-assisted architecture, and results come back as detailed tables, down to the individual event.

Built on

MQT Bench for workloads · pytket-dqc for distribution · Qoala and NetSquid for scheduling and execution, both from TU Delft. Circuit scheduling and entanglement generation no longer rest on in-house heuristics.
